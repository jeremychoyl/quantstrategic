GEKKO MIRROR 150826

A chart mirror of the three strategies running in bridge.py. It shows you what the bots are doing — it does not do any of it.

What it is

Visualisation only. It places no orders, and nothing downstream reads it. Every signal it draws is computed independently on the Mac mini; this is a second opinion rendered on the chart, not the thing making decisions.

That independence is the point. If the Mirror and the bridge ever disagree, one of them has a bug — and you can see it.

Run it on 5-minute only

The EMAs advance once per chart bar. On a 30-minute chart EMA9 becomes roughly an EMA54 in 5-minute terms, and the EMA, EMA9/100 and EMA exit rows will show crosses the bridge never took. The ORB trigger shifts too — it fires on a 30m close instead of the 5m close the bridge acts on.

The D Bias, ATR gate and DC rows are safe at any timeframe; they come from daily data.

Three things that mislead if you don't know them

1 · The three exit rows are not "today"

The daily reset clears the ORB range, flags and side — but not ORB exit, EMA exit or DC exit. Those persist indefinitely until the next exit overwrites them.

So an ORB exit of stop @27997 while price is near 30,200 is not a live number — it is a record from an earlier session. Read all three as "most recent, whenever that was."

2 · "skipped" is different from "traded"

A counter-trend first break sets the same "ORB is finished today" flag as a real entry does. Until 2026-08-16 the table printed done for both, so a dead day and a completed trade were indistinguishable.

They are now separated: traded, skipped, armed.

3 · The bias pair only gates ORB; the ATR gate only gates EMA

They sit next to each other in the table but govern different legs. D Bias decides whether ORB can trade at all today. ATR gate decides whether the EMA is armed. Neither touches DC.

Alerts

Six conditions, all notifications only — no webhook, nothing consumes them. The bridge computes every leg locally.

SlotAlertFires when
plot_9 Gekko ORB entry ORB takes a long or short
plot_10 Gekko ORB skip Counter-trend break — the day is dead
plot_11 Gekko DC entry DC mean-reversion entry
plot_12 Gekko DC exit DC position closes
plot_13 Gekko EMA entry/reverseEMA goes long, short, or reverses either way
plot_14 Gekko EMA exit EMA flattens at end of day
Two rules for these alerts

Never create "Any alert() function call". That is the retired Pickmytrades webhook. The bridge stopped reading it on 2026-07-27 and its message is raw JSON carrying the webhook secret.

Never insert a new condition above an existing one. TradingView binds a saved alert to a plot index, not a name — a live alert stores alert_cond_id: "plot_11". Adding a condition higher up shifts everything below it, and saved alerts silently start firing on the wrong thing while keeping their old names. New conditions go at the bottom.

The EMA condition is deliberately combined across long, short and both reverses. In June it was split per side, only the long alert got created, and the book ran one-sided for weeks. One condition cannot be half-wired. The cost is that the alert can't tell you the direction — check the EMA row.

Where the signals actually come from

Every leg is computed on the Mac mini. TradingView's only job in the live path is feeding bars.

ORB — computed in-process by bridge.py::run_orb, with the trend20 bias from scores.json (Databento). EMA — ema_signal_local.py writing ema_signal_rth.json, RTH basis since 2026-07-27. DC — dc_signal.py from nq_1m.parquet, never had an alert.

Each leg also runs behind an automatic trailing-252-day profit-factor gate. A gated-off leg still logs paper trades but takes no live entries, and reopens by itself when its edge returns.
