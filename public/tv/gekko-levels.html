GEKKO LEVELS

The handful of prices a day trade is actually measured against — and how each one is read alongside the three live strategies.

  • PDH / PDL prior RTH session
  • D CLOSE bias, tinted by side
  • D SMA20
  • ONH / ONL Globex range
  • Opens 09:30 · 18:00 · 00:00

What it is

Nine levels and nine right-edge labels. The labels are reused rather than accumulated, so the object count stays flat however long the chart runs.

It is not a signal source. No orders, no alerts, nothing reads it programmatically. It is context — for discretionary entries, and for judging whether a mechanical signal is walking into an obstacle.

The distinction that matters most

Prior RTH day, not prior daily bar. On CME the daily bar runs 18:00→17:00 ET, so it includes Globex. A daily-bar "previous day high" is usually just the overnight high — a different price from the one an opening-range break tests.

So two conventions are used deliberately: PDH and PDL come from the RTH session only, because that is what ORB breaks. D CLOSE is the daily-bar close at 17:00 ET, not the 16:00 RTH close, because that is the series the bridge's bias reads. Matching the mechanism beats matching convention.

01 · ORB — the bias pair decides whether today trades at all

Bias is set before the open from D CLOSE against D SMA20 and cannot flip intraday. Above → longs only. Below → shorts only. The Mirror's table gives you the verdict; these two lines give you the distance — how close today sits to flipping, which a yes/no readout hides.

Read PDH and ONH before taking an upside break, PDL and ONL before a downside one. Same signal, different room. Where the range edge sits almost on top of an overnight extreme, the break has to clear two levels at once and failures cluster there.

02 · EMA cross — the opens tell you where the series resumes

The averages advance on RTH bars only. The series picks up again at the 09:30 open and ignores the overnight gap entirely — which is why the RTH open level matters more here than it looks.

What the overnight range warns you about

A gap through ONH or ONL is the classic false-cross setup: price gaps, the 09:30 open lands far from EMA200, and the first cross of the session fires into an already-extended move. Seeing the gap measured against the overnight range is the signal that the cross is chasing rather than leading.

03 · DC — the swing leg, and the only one holding overnight

Because DC holds overnight, ONH and ONL stop being trivia and become the gap risk — they are the first prices the next session's inventory has to deal with. PDL is the level to watch on the approach: a daily close below the 10-day low usually happens on a day that has already taken out the prior day's low.

Works on any timeframe — and why that took a fix

Session levels are computed on a fixed 5-minute basis, not on the chart's own bars, so a bar always starts exactly on 09:30, 18:00 and 00:00 ET whatever you are viewing. Five-minute granularity is lossless for session highs and lows, since 5m bars aggregate the minutes inside them.

Detecting sessions from chart bars — what most levels scripts do, and what v1.2 of this one did — breaks silently on any timeframe whose bars don't start on the session edge. Measured on a 1h chart against a known-good 5m read:

Only the two levels touching the 09:30 boundary broke. The overnight range and the 18:00 / 00:00 opens were fine because those edges land on hourly bars, and PDL matched purely by luck — that session's low happened to fall outside the misaligned first hour. Seven of nine levels correct is exactly the kind of bug you trade against for months without noticing.
